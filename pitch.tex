\section*{THE FIVE MINUTES}

\begin{beat}{0:00 -- 0:35\ \ \ THE HOOK}{mint}
\Y\ \say{We are computer science students. Three days ago neither of us could
diagnose anything.}\par\smallskip
\Y\ \say{This is Georges. He is sixty-two, he has had headaches for six weeks,
and he is hiding something. You have four minutes before he dies.}\par\smallskip
\Y\ \say{And the AI playing him has never been told what is wrong with him.
Not told to keep a secret. \textbf{Never told.}}\par\smallskip
\act{Stay on the patient screen. Do not explain the architecture yet --- show the game first.}
\end{beat}

\begin{beat}{0:35 -- 2:35\ \ \ PLAY HIM}{mint}
\Y\ types, one at a time. \A\ reads the room and narrates.\par\smallskip

\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{62mm}X@{}}
\textbf{1.}~\say{is anyone else at home feeling unwell} & \A: \say{Watch the dots. That is a thread of his history.}\\[1.6mm]
\textbf{2.}~\say{do you feel better when you are away from the house} & \A: \say{He does not want to be here. He has an answer ready for everything.}\\[1.6mm]
\textbf{3.}~\say{what heats your house} & \A: \say{He was a boiler engineer for forty years.}\\[1.6mm]
\textbf{4.}~\textbf{Examine} $\rightarrow$ \textbf{Cognition} & \A: \say{His oxygen reads ninety-nine percent. \textbf{That number is lying to you}, and that is not our bug --- a pulse oximeter physically cannot see this.}
\end{tabularx}

\smallskip
\act{\textbf{Diagnose} $\rightarrow$ type \texttt{carbon monoxide} $\rightarrow$ call it. Do not wait for the clock.}\par\smallskip
\A\ (as the reveal lands) \say{His wife and the dog are still in that house.}\par\smallskip
\act{\textbf{Pause here for two seconds.} Let that sit. Do not talk over it.}
\end{beat}

\begin{beat}{2:35 -- 3:50\ \ \ THE PROOF --- this is what wins}{peri}
\A\ \say{Every demo today will tell you their AI does something clever.
We are going to show you ours cannot cheat, and you can check it yourselves.}\par\smallskip
\act{Click \textbf{Show them it couldn't cheat}.}\par\smallskip

\A\ \say{It is not hidden. It was never put in.}\par\smallskip

\section*{THE THREE MINUTES OF QUESTIONS}

\begin{panel}[peri]{Who answers what}
\textbf{Ali} takes anything technical --- architecture, the AI, the proof, the code.\\
\textbf{Yasser} takes the game, the cases, the design, and anything about what is next.\\[1.5mm]
If you do not know: \textbf{say you do not know.} It beats bluffing, every time.
\end{panel}

\bigskip

\qa{How do you know the AI isn't just working out the diagnosis by itself?}{%
\A: It might. A capable model sees yellow eyes and a swollen belly and can infer
liver failure --- we do not claim it is stupid. We claim two things we can prove:
we never gave it the answer, and if it ever says the answer, the player never sees
it. Every reply is scanned before it reaches the screen, and a reply that contains
the answer is thrown away and asked again --- not censored, because a blanked-out
word tells you the word was there.}

\qa{Why not just tell the model to keep it secret?}{%
\A: Because an instruction is negotiable. Everything in a prompt can be argued with,
role-played around, or extracted. Absence cannot. There is no prompt you can write
that makes a model reveal something it does not have.}

\qa{So how is that actually built?}{%
\A: An allowlist. Twelve named fields get copied \textbf{into} the prompt --- name, age,
personality, symptoms, the lie. Five fields are the answer and are never on that list.
The obvious way round is to take the whole file and strip the secrets out, but then a
field somebody adds next year leaks by default. Copying in means a new field is invisible
unless you deliberately add it. It is safe when you forget.}

\qa{How is this different from just role-playing with ChatGPT?}{%
\A: The model only speaks. Everything that makes it a game --- the clock, the vitals,
when he cracks and tells the truth, the scoring --- is our engine, and it is deterministic
and tested. The model never decides anything.}

\qa{Is the medicine accurate?}{%
\Y: We are CS students, so we did not write it from memory. Every case cites published
guidelines --- NICE, the Resuscitation Council, the European Society of Cardiology --- and
they are listed at the end of each case. The vitals thresholds are NEWS2, the score the NHS
actually uses at the bedside.}

\qa{What happens if the wifi dies?}{%
\Y: It keeps playing. There is a full offline patient with scripted replies, and from the
back of a room you cannot tell. That was not a fallback we bolted on --- it is how we built
it before we had an API key.}

\qa{What was the hardest bug?}{%
\A: The guard blocks any word from the diagnosis. One diagnosis was ``liver disease
\textbf{from} untreated hepatitis B'' --- so it banned the word ``from'' and the patient could
not speak an English sentence. We had to add a stopword list. The other one: a naive search
for ``liver'' flags ``de\textbf{liver}ed'', so everything matches on whole words now, and there
is a test with the sentence \say{my wife delivered the news}.}

\qa{What would you do next?}{%
\Y: Three things, in order. More patients --- nine is an hour of play. Then finish the
examinations: about half of them return a generic normal result today. Then multiplayer, which
is already built and tested but not switched on --- a room of phones against one projector.}

\qa{Can a player cheat by looking at the network traffic?}{%
\A: Not during the round --- the answer is in no payload the browser receives, and that is
tested. One honest caveat: the proof panel itself lists the answer words, because that is how
it shows you a hit count of zero. So yes, if you open developer tools mid-round and go
looking for our proof endpoint, you can read them there. We would gate it if this were
shipping.}

\bigskip

\begin{panel}[bubble]{Do not say these}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=5mm,itemsep=1.2mm,topsep=0.8mm]
  \item \textbf{Do not promise his heart spikes when he lies.} It works about half the time --- it is a known bug. If it fires, great, point at it. Never set it up in advance.
  \item \textbf{Do not call it a medical tool.} It is a game that teaches reasoning. Saying otherwise invites a question you cannot win.
  \item \textbf{Do not claim the AI ``cannot know''.} It was never told. That is the honest claim and it is strong enough.
\end{itemize}
\end{panel}

\bigskip

\begin{panel}[lemon]{If something breaks on stage}
\textbf{The AI is slow or silent.} Keep talking, the offline patient answers instantly and nobody can tell.\\[1mm]
\textbf{The 3D room does not load.} Say \say{the room is optional, the game is the conversation} and carry on. It is built to fail that way on purpose.\\[1mm]
\textbf{The page is blank.} Refresh once. The round is on the server, not the browser.\\[1mm]
\textbf{You freeze.} Hand to the other one. That is what two people are for.
\end{panel}
