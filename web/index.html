One encounter, twelve minutes, three things to get right.

A case is a person with a life, a personality, and one thing they would rather not say. The monitor runs the whole time: good questions steady the patient, wasted time makes them worse.

01 — INTERVIEW

Take a history from someone who minimises

Kamal jokes. Rita is defensive. Georges does not like to make a fuss. Ask well and they open up; press the right topic twice and the front drops — and their heart rate gives them away when they lie.

02 — EXAMINE, ORDER, TREAT

Work the case like a real shift

Fourteen examinations, forty investigations with realistic turnaround times, thirty treatments. Key treatments visibly stabilise the monitor. Harmful ones set the patient back — and you find out why in the debrief.

03 — COMMIT, THEN DEBRIEF

An attending marks the whole encounter

Data gathering, clinical management, communication. Every point on the mark sheet is explained. The narrative cites NICE, ESC, AHA, JBDS, GINA, GOLD and RCEM guidance — from a curated registry, so it cannot invent a source.

What makes it different

Most simulators give students a compliant patient and a checklist. Real patients are not compliant, and real reasoning is about what you did not ask.

◐The lie tell

Each patient hides one thing. When they lie about it, their heart rate spikes on the monitor in front of the student. Learning to read a patient, not just a script.

◉Mood that responds to you

Guarded, defensive, rattled, scared, pleading, resigned. The patient's state is engine-derived from the vitals and how hard they have been pressed — and steers the model's tone.

▤Debriefs that cannot fabricate

The grader chooses citations by id from a registry of published guidelines. If a source is not in the registry, it cannot be cited. Every mark is reproducible without a model at all.

▥Instructor dashboard

Cohorts, assignments with due dates, per-learner trends, per-case difficulty, the most-missed questions across your whole year group, and every debrief on record.

✎Case editor with AI drafting

Describe a case in a sentence; get a complete, validated draft: history, personality, lies, findings, results, teaching points. Edit anything. Publish only when it passes the same checks as ours.

▣Classroom mode

One projector, a room full of phones, one patient, a 150-second clock. A campaign of three patients with a leaderboard. The way to open a teaching session.

⚙The answer never reaches the model

The diagnosis, the accepted answers and the key questions are never in a prompt. The prompt is built from an allowlist of fields; anything else cannot leak. An output guard checks every reply anyway. Tested on every commit with 100 injection attacks.

◌Works offline, works everywhere

No model key? The patient has a scripted voice from the same material, and the debrief still marks every point. Vanilla web, one small server, SQLite — runs on a laptop in a lecture theatre or in Docker on a VM.

Questions

Is this a medical device or clinical decision support?

No. Ya Hakim is an education tool with synthetic patients. Nothing it produces is medical advice, and it should never be used for a real patient.

How accurate are the cases?

Built-in cases are written to current published guidance and validated automatically: vitals, keyword coverage, leak checks. Schools can review, edit and replace any case, and the debrief cites the guidance it marks against so faculty can check every claim.

What happens to student data?

Transcripts and debriefs are stored in your school's own database and visible to your instructors. Patient prompts contain no student identifiers. Self-hosting is available for institutions with residency requirements.

Do we need an AI key?

For the live patient, the narrative debrief and AI case drafting, yes — an Anthropic API key on the server. Without one the product still runs end to end with scripted patients and checklist debriefs, which is also what our CI runs.

Can we write our own cases?

Yes. Draft one from a sentence, or start from a template. A case is a JSON document with a documented schema; it publishes only when it passes the same validation as the built-in cases.
